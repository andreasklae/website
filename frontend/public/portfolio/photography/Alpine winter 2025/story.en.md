# Alpine Winter 2025

Fog, mountains, and a life lived in transit. From a quiet Bavarian valley to Milan’s warm evenings, and finally the stillness of lakes tucked between the Alps.

## Chapter 1 – Berchtesgaden

We climbed toward the Eagle’s Nest, Hitlers old cabin, in thick fog, the kind that muffles sound and turns the world into a small circle around your feet. Every now and then it opened just enough to reveal a slice of the Alps. A sudden drop, a ridge line, a hint of the valley, before the white swallowed it again. At the top we caught those glimpses like stolen frames, standing there tired and a little breathless, mostly in awe.

We stayed in a small loft at the bottom of the valley, with a balcony view that made the mountains feel like walls around the day. We also went to Königssee in the off-season. Everything was closed, the restaurants empty, and the place had the quiet of a set after the actors have left. You could tell summer would be packed with tours and crowds, but in November it felt like a ghost town, fog hanging over the water and silence doing most of the talking.

## Chapter 2 – Salzburg

Salzburg was a day trip, a quick shift from valley quiet to city rhythm. We wandered through old streets and open squares, taking in palaces, castles, and buildings that seemed to carry their age with confidence. The best moments were the viewpoints: climbing, turning a corner, and watching the city arrange itself below. Rooftops, spires, and the surrounding hills dissolving into haze. It didn’t feel dead the way the lakes did; cafés were still warm with people, even on an ordinary weekday.

## Chapter 3 – Milano

Milan was evenings that went on without rushing. Aperitivo by the canal in Navigli then dinner somewhere packed and loud in the best way. Pasta, red wine, and the kind of atmosphere that makes you forget the weather outside. Dessert always meant tiramisu, followed by espresso as if it was punctuation. A local friend showed us the city in that casual way that makes it feel less like a destination and more like a place you could belong. One night we ended up at a bar, rowdy and crowded, where someone ordered an absurd 8 litre glass cannon of beer to share. It wasn’t fancy, but it was exactly right. Strangers and friends folded into the same loud, easy energy.

## Chapter 4 – Italian and Swiss Lakes

From Milan we traded streets for water and steep slopes. Lake Como gave us a funicular ride into higher air, where the view kept expanding as we climbed. More lake, more rooftops, more mountain behind mountain. Later we drove toward Lago di Lugano and stayed in Albogasio, right across the border from Switzerland. Mornings started at a local café, then we spent the day moving village to village along the lake, stopping whenever the scenery demanded it. Some places were old and Italian, pressed right up against the water. Others were Swiss towns sat improbably higher on the mountain, with a town hall, schools, and everyday life perched among steep slopes like it had always been there. In the evening we wandered Lugano. It was bigger, brighter, but still carrying that lakeside charm, and found a Christmas market glowing against the cold. It felt like the perfect ending to a trip that never stayed still for long: fog lifting into views, cities warming into nights, and lakes reflecting the Alps at a distance.
